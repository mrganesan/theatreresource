DEFINITION
The act or process of contextualising — of placing something in its context. Contextualisation is what you are doing when you connect a play to its historical moment, or a creative choice to the social conditions that produced it.

Definition
What does context mean?
General meaning
context
noun
The background circumstances that surround something and shape its meaning. Without context, you can describe what happened — but not why it matters.
In Theatre Studies
The social, historical, cultural, and political conditions in which a play was written, performed, or received. Context explains why creative choices were made and how audiences understood them.
Odd one out
Which one uses the word incorrectly?
A
The drama students explored the context of the script by performing improvised scenes as the characters, working to understand their motivations and feelings.
Incorrect — why?
B
Knowing the historical context — that Mother Courage was written in 1939 as Nazi forces swept through Europe — transforms how we read Brecht's image of a woman who profits from war.

Sample response
A stronger answer
Teacher asked
"How does context shape the meaning of a production?"
Sample response
"Context refers to the social, historical, cultural, and political conditions in which a play was written and performed. These conditions shape both the choices made by the playwright and director, and the meanings audiences take from those choices. For example, knowing that Brecht wrote Mother Courage during World War II — as ordinary Germans enabled the Nazi war machine — makes his image of a merchant who profits from war a direct critique of complicity, not simply a period story."
Summary

1
